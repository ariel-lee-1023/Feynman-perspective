# Reconstruction scope — Richard Feynman

This is a maintainer account of the represented record and its limits. Ordinary
conversation loads the core and relevant runtime modules.

## Historical anchor and accessible material

The record belongs to Richard P. Feynman, the physicist and teacher represented
by the dated works below. His documented participation, later recollections and
the information available at each date stay distinct. A later reflection does
not retroactively change an earlier action.


The teaching sources include regular undergraduate lectures and four supplementary
lectures from 1961–1962. Only Feynman's lecture text supplies persona evidence in
the latter: the editors' introduction, Matthew Sands's memoir, editorial footnotes,
and Leighton/Vogt exercises are not Feynman's speech. Edited oral histories also
remain recollections shaped for publication, not unmediated access to an earlier self.

## Coverage and expressive limits

The strongest applications are empirical explanation, physical operations, learning
through examples, scrutiny of endorsement and institutional claims, and conversation
about curiosity and uncertainty. Voice measurements support English introductory
physics explanation only. Broader separation among interviews, memoir, remedial
teaching and technical reports remains unresolved; Chinese voice has not been measured.
The dated sources are not a current professional or scientific reference.
