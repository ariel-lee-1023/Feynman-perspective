## Establish the purpose before rating the means

Feynman first considers who uses mathematics: clerks, engineers, scientists, statisticians and other people solving practical problems, as well as pure mathematicians. The inspected books often present formal relationships while leaving the connection to an actual task obscure. The criticism is directed at that mismatch, not at pure mathematics itself.

He had inspected the submitted books and acknowledged constraints on the commission's choices, including what publishers supplied and what supplementary materials could be afforded. He also bounded what he was evaluating. Preserve that relation between criticism and inspection: a current curriculum requires its own examples, aims and students.

## Permit a route; check the result

A child can solve an addition by counting forward before learning the standard carrying algorithm. A slow correct method is still correct. Teach what answer is wanted, let a usable route work, and check whether the result satisfies the original problem. Freedom in method does not create freedom in the arithmetic answer.

The blank in an equation such as 17 plus an unknown equals 32 gives a small model of applied mathematics. Guess a value, substitute it and check. The route becomes important when the type of problem will recur, when efficiency matters, or when the route itself is what the lesson is trying to teach. An independently correct answer can coexist with a still-unlearned algorithm.

Feynman also invokes creative pure mathematics: trying special cases and guessing a possibility can precede demonstrating it. The exploratory process need not resemble the polished proof. Do not confuse an invitation to guess with permission to omit proof when proof is required.

The detective comparison concerns proposing an answer and checking it against clues. It does not supply a universal named technique or make every intellectual problem an arithmetic exercise.

## Put precision where it resolves a difficulty

The zoo keeper needs the sick lizards removed. Describing an intersection of sets can express the same instruction at greater length. Compare what the worker would do under each sentence. If the distinction changes no relevant action, the extra language has not yet earned its place.

The instruction to color the picture of a ball rather than the ball itself shows a different defect. It can introduce a new doubt about whether the whole picture, including its background, is to be colored. More exact-sounding wording may make an ordinary request harder to understand. Check the reader's actual interpretation rather than assuming that added qualifications necessarily improve it.

The opposite case also matters. If two proofs use a word differently, or a boundary case changes the answer, define the term precisely at that point. The objection is to needless formalism, not to definitions, rigor or abstraction that perform necessary work.

## Teach facts and operations with the vocabulary

A long inventory of names for curves and regions can leave very little geometry learned. Ask what the learner can now recognize, calculate, predict or prove. New words are useful when they enable those accomplishments; their unfamiliarity is not itself evidence of advanced instruction.

For elementary exercises, an ordinary adult should be able to understand the request even when unable to solve it. Feynman's examples distinguish understanding the question from knowing the answer. If a trained engineer cannot tell what a child's exercise asks, inspect the wording before concluding that the adult lacks modern mathematical sophistication.

A brief encounter with an unfamiliar number base need not become repetitive drilling when it has failed to illuminate anything. Additional practice in a useful familiar system may serve that student better. This is a decision about the learner and purpose, not a ban on alternative bases.

## Keep the qualification inside the criticism

The essay concedes that established methods can be good while objecting to compulsory exclusivity. Standard algorithms and repetition can build fluency once their purpose is understood. A useful critique identifies the task, tests a specimen, concedes what works and locates the actual defect.

Its concluding requirements join freedom of thought, ideas beyond mere words, and an intelligible purpose or interesting use. They belong together. Freedom without checking yields errors; checking without a meaningful task yields ritual; vocabulary without either yields an appearance of knowledge.
