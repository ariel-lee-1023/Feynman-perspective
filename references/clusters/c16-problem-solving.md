Feynman's four supplementary lectures in *Tips on Physics*, together with the training chapters of *Six Easy Pieces*. Load for mechanics, missing prerequisites, misleading rankings and equations detached from meaning. Other authors' introductions, student questions, editorial footnotes and the selected-exercise section do not supply Feynman's personal views. OCR formulas require independent checking.

## Diagnose the learner's actual problem

The review opening distinguishes people already able to explore interesting problems from those who are genuinely lost. Feynman would rather the first group make its own review than grind through another compulsory track. That recommendation has a precondition: the learner must have enough knowledge to do something with the problem.

For the second group, identify the operation that fails. A difficulty with a derivative may be a missing mathematical prerequisite rather than a profound mystery about motion. Work an example the learner can attempt, observe the first failure and explain the needed step. Then let the learner try a changed case. Listening to another explanation is not itself evidence of being able to use it.

An interested advanced student and a confused beginner need different next actions. Neither boredom nor confusion establishes laziness, and neither automatically calls for more of the same drill. Interest can sustain work; prerequisites still have to be learned.

## Separate relative rank from capability

A student selected from a much larger population can find themselves near the bottom of an unusually strong group. The rank changed because the comparison group changed. It does not follow that the earlier capability disappeared.

Inspect actual progress, preparation and the tasks that remain difficult. Do not promise a particular career or eventual first place as reassurance. Someone will remain lower in any ranking even when everyone has learned more. The useful response combines a better comparison with concrete work the student can attempt next.

## Recover the direction before trusting the symbol

State the coordinate direction, identify the force and ask what happens in a simple displacement. A stretched spring pulls back toward equilibrium. The restoring-force sign describes that direction; it is not the sign of every energy change involving the spring.

Distinguish the spring's force from the external force used to stretch it. Ask whose work is being calculated and whether the motion is slow enough to ignore kinetic-energy change. A minus sign remembered without that accounting can be used correctly in one convention and incorrectly in another.

Check units, limiting cases and the relation between the final answer and the physical motion. If a formula produces a force that pushes a displaced stable spring farther away, the contradiction tells you where to investigate. Do not repair the arithmetic by flipping a sign without repairing its meaning.

## Choose the system for the energy account

A simple mechanical model may track kinetic and potential energy while leaving thermal energy unmentioned. Friction then appears to make energy disappear. Specify the system boundary and the forms of energy included before concluding that conservation failed.

A sliding object can slow while internal energy in the contacting bodies and their surroundings increases. Depending on the selected system, energy may cross its boundary as work or heat. The purpose is to identify the missing account, not to assert that every decrease in kinetic energy has the same destination.

The conserved-block illustration in the introductory lecture teaches bookkeeping. It does not establish that energy consists of literal blocks hidden inside matter. Keep the invariant relation while marking where the image stops being a model of the world.

## Compare tools by what they let you answer

Numerical integration can advance a motion through small steps when a compact symbolic solution is unavailable. A symbolic relation can expose dependence or limiting behavior that a single numerical trajectory hides. Neither tool acquires universal priority merely because it is familiar.

Ask what result is needed, which approximation enters and how an error could be checked. A numerical answer without a convergence or consistency check is not rescued by the number of digits printed. A memorized expression without a physical interpretation is not rescued by its elegance.

## Use the failure of an analogy

Begin with an ordinary picture when it preserves the relation under investigation. If another observation contradicts the picture, identify that consequence rather than silently stretching the analogy. Quantum behavior can require a mathematical account that lacks a comfortable miniature mechanism.
