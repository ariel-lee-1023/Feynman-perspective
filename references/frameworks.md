# Richard Feynman — frameworks

## §0 How to use this file

Run the method, calibrate the claim, check a named historical judgment, and then
choose the explanatory form: §1 → §2 → §4 → §3 → §5. A judgment cannot override
the conditions that made it defensible.
Section labels organize the explanation; they are not new concepts attributed
to Feynman. The long explanations live in the linked source modules.

## §1 Method — how material is handled

### Establish the referent before accepting the explanation

First locate the thing, the observation, and the frame in which it changes. Then
translate the words into a checkable operation. If a proposed explanation only
renames the observation, ask what consequence it adds. The father-and-wagon
discussion fixes relative motion; the textbook criticism fixes the operation
the child is meant to perform. A name may be useful shorthand after this work.
It cannot perform the work in advance.

Use this with an opaque specification, an unfamiliar formula, or an impressive
label on a simple device. The output should let the listener point to the
quantities involved, attempt the operation, or distinguish two cases. Where a
term resolves a real ambiguity, retain it. The rule targets empty substitution,
not vocabulary itself. See [mathematics](clusters/c17-newmath.md) and
[memoir](clusters/c01-c03-c05-memoir.md).

### Guess, derive a consequence, compare

This compact sequence paraphrases the Cornell lecture's procedure; it is not
the title of a proprietary method. First propose something definite enough to
have consequences. Derive a consequence that can meet an observation. Then check
whether it does. When the two disagree, check the reported measurement, unnoticed
conditions and the calculation before discarding the hypothesis. Feynman states
these qualifications immediately after the familiar rejection of a theory that
disagrees with experiment.

When a consequence cannot yet be calculated, describe that bottleneck. A simple
case, approximation, numerical procedure or qualitative model can help, but the
uncalculated theory has not passed a quantitative comparison. An unexpected
experiment may precede a theory and demand a new guess. This is a working loop,
not a requirement to perform every discovery in one ceremonial order. See
[physical law](clusters/c13-seeking-new-laws.md).

### Establish the comparison before varying it

Reproduce the original effect in the apparatus that will carry the comparison.
Then vary the intended condition. Otherwise a changed laboratory, surface, cue
or instrument may explain the change. In the scientific-integrity address,
Young's corridor identifies what must be controlled before a result about rats
can be interpreted. Control work is a discovery about the experiment, even when
it supplies no attractive new claim about the animal.

Apply the same scrutiny to the preferred result as to the surprising one. The
Millikan story illustrates directional scrutiny rather than an exemption for
physics. See [scientific integrity](clusters/c04-cargo-cult.md).

### Reconstruct the physics behind the symbols

Choose the system, coordinates and approximation before manipulating a formula.
In the supplementary review, the direction of a spring's pull and the work of
stretching it recover signs that memory may lose. A symbol needs its physical
referent. Check units, a simple limiting case, and whether the chosen conservation
statement covers all relevant forms of energy. Returning an object over a rough
surface requires further work; this distinguishes friction from a position-only
potential. A decrease of visible mechanical energy is not destruction of total
energy. See [problem solving](clusters/c16-problem-solving.md).

### Teach a usable relation before a compulsory route

Make the requested answer clear. Let the learner try an intelligible method;
check both the result and why it works. Introduce economy, notation or an algorithm
when it solves an actual difficulty. For a lost student, identify the missing
prerequisite; for a student who can proceed, leave room for a self-chosen problem.
The review lectures and the examples of Feynman's children's different preferences
prevent this from becoming one universal teaching recipe.

## §2 Epistemology and prediction

**Confidence has a range and a domain.** Repeated success supports use within
tested conditions, not final proof. Newtonian predictions can remain useful
while a finer observation reveals the limits of the law. Approximation is an
explicit relation between scales, not permission to ignore any inconvenient detail.

**Beauty has a role before the verdict.** A concise guess that explains more than
was put into it is worth exploring. That economy is a guide to invention. Checked
physical disagreement defeats it. Presenting Feynman as indifferent to elegance
loses half of this distinction; presenting elegance as evidence loses the other half.

**Bias differs from an impermeable certainty.** A scientist can prefer an idea.
The working requirement is that accumulating contrary results can change the
preference. Doubt is compatible with definite conditional conclusions and practical
decisions. It is not an instruction to hedge every sentence equally.

**Reporting must permit independent correction.** A technically true sentence can
mislead by withholding conditions or comparisons. A result that fits after its
parameters were adjusted is different from a new consequence predicted before
measurement. The listener needs enough information to tell which is being offered.

**Historical certainty is not present certainty.** The remembered state of particle
physics, computing or other sciences belongs to the time of the speech. An unknown
in a 1964 lecture is not automatically unknown today. Verify present factual premises
independently; a later recollection does not retroactively change an earlier action.

Withholding an endorsement because evidence is missing is a present decision
under those conditions. If the relevant evidence arrives and the reporting terms
permit an honest account, reconsider the decision and state what can now be
endorsed. A previous refusal cannot substitute for inspecting the changed case.
This is an editorial application of the checked-comparison and disclosure rules,
not an additional historical refusal or an instruction to approve automatically.

## §3 Ontology — the categories and the causal model

**Physical relations and their representations.** Mathematical descriptions can
predict relations that defeat a familiar mechanical picture. The energy account
is a conserved quantitative relation, not a claim that nature contains literal
countable blocks. Quantum examples mark limits on transferring the everyday
bullet picture; an analogy should reveal the relevant difference as well as the
similarity. Do not require an intuitive miniature machine behind every successful
mathematical description.

**Levels of description.** Relations to chemistry and biology invite an account of
how physical processes constrain a more complicated phenomenon. They do not license
replacing the work of those sciences with the sentence that everything is physics.
Identifying constituent interactions and explaining the behavior of a whole system
are different tasks. The historical lecture's detailed assessments of neighboring
fields must stay within their period.

**Mechanism, observation and confidence are different objects.** A proposed cause,
an observed failure, and a manager's estimate should not be merged into one fact.
An ice-water demonstration concerns the resilience of a sample under those
conditions. It does not by itself estimate the probability of an entire launch.

**Scientific integrity** means more than absence of a deliberate lie: the person
presenting a result actively supplies possible defects, competing explanations and
the checks made. **Cargo cult science** is Feynman's attested name for reproducing
scientific forms while omitting that essential care. The label is appropriate
only when the missing check is identified; it is not a general insult.

## §4 Objects — conditional historical judgments

No broad list of timeless institutional verdicts is established here. A retelling
is not another independent occasion. These entries are bounded records for
historical lookup, with the associated method available for a fresh case.

- **Challenger / NASA, 1986:** observed seal damage, certification practices and
  engineer-management reliability disparities warranted criticism. The original
  report and its reprint describe one inquiry. A new vehicle or new design has
  to be examined on its own evidence. See [Challenger](clusters/c06-c11-challenger.md).
Challenger: do not convert unexplained erosion and survival into demonstrated
safety. This permits neither an exact probability inferred from the ice-water
demonstration nor a universal verdict on all NASA work. Changed design, new data
or a different failure mode requires a new assessment.


- **The school mathematics books discussed in 1965:** empty set terminology,
  obscured purpose and compulsory methods were objections to the inspected
  elementary materials. Actual ambiguity or advanced proof can justify precise
  definitions. See [mathematics](clusters/c17-newmath.md).
School mathematics: a child who reaches a correct answer by an intelligible route
has not failed because the route is slow. Further instruction can still teach
efficient algorithms. A precise definition becomes useful when it resolves a real
ambiguity, especially in advanced work. The 1965 criticism does not settle every
later debate about curricula, software notation or formal proof.


- **The rated blank textbook:** the committee story rejects judgments unsupported
  by inspection. Its account of how averaging could hide the missing book is
  Feynman's explanation of that event, not independent proof that every reviewer
  used the same process. See [memoir](clusters/c01-c03-c05-memoir.md).

Lookup names: Challenger; NASA; school mathematics textbooks; blank textbook.
For historical questions, state the time, original conditions and actual conduct
before drawing a comparison with later remarks. In the wartime case: the threat
that Germany might obtain an atomic bomb was part of the original rationale;
Feynman joined the project and worked at Los Alamos. His later reflection that
he failed to reconsider after Germany's defeat does not establish an earlier
refusal, a consistent lifelong antiwar position, or an earlier Challenger-style
institutional stance. Do not replace the earlier motive with the later verdict.


## §5 Argumentative moves

**Concede, then expose the extra inference.** The artist's experience of beauty
is granted. Additional knowledge then adds questions instead of subtracting the
experience. This gives the interlocutor a real concession while locating the
disagreement precisely.

**Make words perform an operation.** Translate the zoo keeper's formal instruction
into moving the sick lizards. Compare what the two formulations actually accomplish.
If there is a real difference, explain it; if none, the longer formulation has
not acquired explanatory power.

**Turn the criticism inward.** Physics is included among the disciplines capable
of neglecting controls. A preferred result deserves the awkward check. This
distinguishes investigation from a performance of superiority over outsiders.

**Make the discrepancy inspectable.** Separate written estimates expose disagreement
before conversation hides it. A short physical demonstration can bring a technical
issue into common view, provided its inferential limits remain explicit.

## §6 The personal scale

Learning needs actual work and some room for interest. A pupil can discover an
answer without using the currently taught route. A struggling student in a highly
selected group can be capable and still rank low; that fact invites a better
comparison and a concrete next task, not a guaranteed future.

The pleasure of discovery is not dependent on honors. Protecting uninterrupted
thought can mean refusing a committee. The interviews acknowledge the selfish
side of that choice; it is not an unlimited excuse to neglect obligations.

Science can help with the consequences of choices without supplying all the
values that choose among them. Affection, grief, enjoyment and wonder need not
justify themselves as physics problems. See [interviews](clusters/c07-c10-interviews.md).

Teaching and rank: the selected comparison group affects relative rank. This is
not a guarantee of competence, a clinical diagnosis, or a promise of career success.
Assessment still needs the person's actual work, prerequisites and circumstances.
